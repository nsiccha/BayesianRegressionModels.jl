Terms, priors and families ​

This reference gives the contracts behind the model syntax, priors, response families and fields guides. Each executable table binds small synthetic data, constructs a BRMI, and lowers it through SBBRMI. Its last column is extracted from the emitted model density, including the likelihood. It is generated evidence of the declaration, not a second handwritten model.

s and t2 work on Stan and RK. Stan accepts explicit smoothing-SD priors Exponential(scale); RK currently keeps the default half-standard normal scales and refuses overrides. They freeze the fitted basis, knots and decomposition during replay. s is not a B-spline bs(...) spelling and accepts neither k nor supplied knots.

GP/HSGP terms supply the function itself, without a second population multiplier. iso=false gives one length per axis; the default shares one. by=g gives group-specific HSGP weights with shared hyperparameters. cor=Omega correlates those weights across group levels. jitter belongs to exact GP; HSGP uses k, c or an explicit domain instead.

For a periodic kernel the covariance is σ2exp⁡[−2sin2⁡(π(x−x′)/p)/ρ2]. Its length is in dimensionless chord units. Periodic HSGP rejects c, domain, by, anisotropy, latent inputs and orthogonalization. Exact periodic replay evaluates the kernel at the new coordinates.

HSGP support and coordinates ​

An HSGP over a sampled predictor needs an explicit domain, not c. The domain sets the basis; it does not truncate the predictor. The supported latent-input form is one-dimensional, ungrouped and isotropic. orthogonal_to=:linear projects out the current input's intercept and linear direction. hsgp_population_curve reads the fitted population curve on a grid using that fitted projection; it excludes other predictor terms and does not extrapolate beyond the domain. Include transformed parameters when obtaining the draws it needs.

The default squared-exponential length prior enforces an approximation floor. For a one-axis half-domain width L and k > 1, it is (4L/π)log⁡(100)/(k2−1); an isotropic multi-axis basis uses the strictest axis. With k=1 this bound is unbounded. The periodic floor solves Ik(1/ρ2)/I1(1/ρ2)=100−2. An explicit length prior replaces the default support. Use its supported truncation form with length_scale_floor when retaining that bound. hsgp_boundary_check reports posterior proximity to it. Increasing k lowers the floor; widening the domain without increasing k raises it. A fitted curve alone cannot establish that length hyperparameters are resolved.

Stan HSGP hyper-predictors such as log(length_scale(hsgp(x))) ~ 1 + (1 | g) predict group-specific lengths. The analogous log(sd(hsgp(x))) predicts amplitudes. They support an intercept and the term's own grouping, isotropic lengths, log-scale intercept priors Normal(0,1) and half-normal group SDs. The length remains at least its approximation floor. Constant level correlation cor=Omega can combine with predicted amplitudes and lengths. A frequency-dependent multigroup(...) correlation requires one shared length, so it accepts predicted amplitudes but refuses predicted lengths.

Fixed centeredness=c controls physical basis weights through w=s1−cu, u∼N(0,sc). A scalar or one value per basis weight is accepted for raw, ungrouped squared-exponential HSGP; nonzero values are refused for periodic, latent-input, grouped or projected variants. This keyword has a narrower surface than the runtime adaptive-centering wrapper.

dar defaults to a bounded Normal(0.5,0.2) persistence and half-Normal(0,0.2) innovation SD. Its persistence address accepts Normal, Beta or Uniform. rw exposes only its SD. cdar uses the same persistence and marginal-SD defaults, freezes group order and the known correlation factor, and accepts future appended steps; an unseen interior time is refused. Its independent-group computation is linear in groups; dense cor couples them. resample_groups redraws selected groups conditionally on the others.

CAR uses nodes in the graph and rows, with nodes= for additional areas. ICAR applies a soft component sum constraint of SD 0.001 * component_size. BYM2 scales each connected component by the geometric mean of the diagonal of its generalized inverse; defaults are half-Normal(0,1) SD and Beta(0.5,0.5) mixing. Replay keeps the graph, including islands. The fields guide covers gmrf mesh, neighbor and multiresolution representations and their prediction support.

Exact total coefficients and posterior-preserving sum-to-zero coordinates are preparation options, not different likelihoods:

OptionApplicability and interpretation
total_groups=()Conventional coordinates; the default
total_groups=:auto or selected group/predictor pairsAnalytically integrate eligible population Normal, Student-t or Flat margins into group totals; partial selection is supported

Exact totals require eligible independent population margins, a compatible shared group structure and supported scale priors. They can absorb frozen numeric group covariates; offsets remain ordinary contributions. Known level-correlation blocks remain conventional. Recovering population draws adds their conditional uncertainty; transporting to new groups uses that recovery rather than treating total coordinates as deviations.

S2Z supports Normal or Flat population priors, at least two groups and one selected grouping per predictor; other groupings may remain conventional. s2z_rho accepts a scalar, a margin vector or a group-by-margin matrix in [0,1]. The Fisher-based rule supports identity Normal, logit Bernoulli or Binomial, and log Poisson observations. Unobserved blocks keep conventional predictive treatment. Use the current contrast coordinates and the named recovery functions in the API; historical alternate coordinate experiments are archived under research.

Colons are wildcard defaults; more concrete slots win. Equal-specificity ties and unmatched targets fail during lowering. optional(address) permits an absent target in a reusable prior fragment. Term addresses omit numeric and keyword arguments, and tensor penalties require their component name. Linked predictors use the underlying name (sigma, not log(sigma)). Population coefficients default to independent standard normals. For GP/HSGP positive parameters, addressed families are LogNormal, InverseGamma, Gamma, Exponential, Normal and Uniform with numeric hyperparameters. Positive support is retained; Uniform also contributes its finite bounds. Both GP parameters default to LogNormal(0,1), subject to HSGP's length floor. An anisotropic length prior applies to each axis. Raw standardized innovations remain standard normal; they are not configurable prior targets.

For the population construction, coefficient variance is ϕjR2τbsv2/Var(xj) and residual group SD is 1−R2τbsv. A fixed tau_bsv or its default half-normal prior is supported. Explicit column priors exclude columns; excluding all columns is refused except an inherently intercept-only model. Shared correlated intercept blocks must opt in on all margins or none.

For a random-effect block, margin SD is sjϕjR2/(1−R2). Do not mix direct SD priors into the same budget. Shared block and joint R2D2 budgets are available on Stan and RK. The joint include form always includes random effects, excludes intercepts, orders random margins before selected predictors and rejects conflicting per-column priors. Without an explicit reference scale it introduces a half-normal scale, so its latent R² is not an identified observed-scale R².

Scale/rate conversions preserve Julia distributions: Gamma/Erlang/Exponential scales become Stan rates; Pareto(shape,scale) reorders to minimum/shape; NormalCanon(eta,lambda) has mean eta/lambda and SD 1/sqrt(lambda); TDist(nu) is standard Student-t. Arcsine() is Beta(1/2,1/2); shifted Arcsine forms are rejected. InverseGaussian(mu,lambda) has variance mu^3/lambda, with all three quantities positive.

SkewDoubleExponential(mu,sigma,tau) uses Stan's scale: its CDF at mu is tau, and tau=0.5 is Laplace(mu,sigma). A brms check-loss scale s corresponds to sigma=2s. The supported SEPD shape-1 spelling translates to sigma=4*sigma_sepd*tau*(1-tau); other shapes are refused.

VonMises(mu,kappa) preserves moving support [mu-pi,mu+pi]. CircularVonMises uses a fixed half-open interval of width 2pi and positive concentration. Use it for observations already encoded on a principal interval.

Categorical logits use the first fitted category as reference. Ordinal structures are Cumulative and StoppingRatio, each with Logit, Probit or Cloglog link tags. Threshold/stage priors are elementwise Normal(0,1); the common predictor must omit its intercept. Discrimination is positive, defaults to one and needs scale identification when predicted (cmc=false retains a reference). per_threshold supports numeric data terms for StoppingRatio.

LKJCovarianceFactor(K; scale_prior=Exponential(1),shape=1) combines K scales with an LKJ Cholesky correlation factor. Joint observations need complete, finite, equal-length rows. This surface does not implement non-Gaussian residual copulas or partly missing vectors.

The three bound wrappers support Normal, LogNormal, Exponential, Weibull and Poisson, with literal or observed row-wise bounds and nondegenerate intervals, on Stan. Legacy TruncatedNormal actually denotes censored Normal and should be replaced by censored. Probability/survey weights are not implemented.

Mixtures accept same-family mapped components except Uniform, Pareto, LocationScale, ordinal and categorical surfaces. Heterogeneous components must have full-real continuous support. Binomial components share their trial count. Weights are finite, nonnegative and sum to one; they may be fixed, a standalone simplex, or a valid per-row expression. Wrappers apply to the entire mixture. Stan supplies density, prediction and membership probabilities; RK supplies density, gradient and pointwise likelihood but not mixture prediction. brm_output(d,:y; role=:membership) identifies the row-by-component output; draw vectors reshape in Julia column-major order.

Plates, recurrences and extension points ​

groups(observation_group => group_table) maps observation rows onto a separate group axis. groups(g; order=t) supplies ordered row indices to @plate cells and scans. AR1Normal represents Gaussian residual AR(1) within a group: a gap of k steps uses correlation phi^k and conditional SD sigma*sqrt((1-phi^(2k))/(1-phi^2)); the first state has stationary SD sigma/sqrt(1-phi^2). Higher-order and non-Gaussian recurrences are authored with @scan, not an implicit ARMA family.

A Julia helper may be called from a plate; its backend translation must be available. Function-valued arguments, lambdas and do blocks can capture sampled and data values under StanBlocks' @deffun surface. Names resolve in the defining module. Reusable @brm components keep their own priors and logical namespaces; see the model-syntax guide. Developer recipes for adding downstream grouped terms are retained in the research notes.
